How TrailBlazing reads IGN data

Paths come live from IGN's BD TOPO (road section layer, troncon_de_route) through the Géoplateforme. Zoom in to level 14 or more to load them.

Green: section whose IGN nature is Chemin, Sentier, Route empierrée or Escalier, with no "private" flag and no "reserved to authorised users" access. These are the best candidates for chemins ruraux (old chemins vicinaux), which are open to public use (Code rural, art. L161-1 and following).

Red dotted: IGN marks the section as private or reserved to authorised users (Restreint aux ayants droit).

Limits. IGN does not record legal ownership, so a green track can still be a private chemin d'exploitation, and a mayor can restrict motor vehicles by local order. Turn on the Cadastre overlay: a path running between parcels is usually public, one crossing inside a numbered parcel is usually private. Always respect signs on the ground.

Cadastre check (zoom 15+): the app loads the cadastral parcels (Parcellaire Express) and tests each green path. Roads and chemins ruraux are normally left out of the numbered parcels, so a path running in the gap between parcels is shown dark green (public). A path lying inside a numbered parcel is shown orange dashed: it belongs to that parcel's owner, usually private, sometimes the commune. BD TOPO and the cadastre can be offset by a few metres, so points within 4 m of a parcel edge count as "between".

Access restrictions (zoom 15+), for the travel mode you pick:
• OpenStreetMap access tags recorded by volunteers from signs on the ground (motor_vehicle=no, access=private, agricultural, forestry, seasonal rules) and barriers (gates, bollards, stiles);
• protected areas (national park cores, nature reserves, biotope protection orders) and public forests (ONF), where motor vehicles are usually banned off open roads.
Local bans by the mayor or the prefect (arrêtés) are not published in any national database: they are only displayed at the mairie and on signs.
